# TOM / VRO Methodology

A portable operating-model + value-realization method. Two connected workstreams:
the **TOM** defines *how teams will work differently* once the new system is live; the
**VRO** measures *whether* those changes deliver the funded business value. Without TOM
work, teams adopt the system but not the new ways of working, and the benefits never
materialize; without VRO tracking, there's no feedback loop.

## The 6-step method
1. **Ingest** — read the CIA + fit-gaps/transcripts into a structured baseline (functional areas, impact counts, roles, severity).
2. **Map** — per functional area, map the lifecycle and the cross-functional value streams; find where the system changes inputs, outputs, participants, timing, and decisions.
3. **Reshape** — assess the future state across the 8 TOM dimensions; test coherence across them.
4. **Prioritize** — rank functional areas by people-impact density & severity.
5. **Surface** — the new roles/seats, the value-at-risk from open gaps, and the AI role-shifts.
6. **Generate** — the accelerator slide, TOM approach doc, VRO registers, and dashboard — reconciled to the CIA.

## The 8 TOM dimensions
Assess the future state on each; the dimensions are **interdependent** (a change in one only holds if the others move with it — test each functional-area design for coherence across all eight).

1. **Meetings & Decision Cadence** — which forums shorten/eliminate; what new ones (e.g. S&OP) are needed.
2. **Roles & Responsibilities** — who owns data at each stage gate; what new seats the model implies.
3. **Data & Reporting** — what becomes self-service; what data-quality gates are needed.
4. **Cross-Functional Coordination** — how shared items and cross-BU handoffs work in one system.
5. **Skills & Capabilities** — where teams upskill from execution to system governance (beyond tool training).
6. **Governance & Escalation** — how the change network operates post go-live; escalation paths.
7. **Guest / Customer & Service Outcomes** — which service-level outcomes the model improves, and how they're measured.
8. **Locations & Sourcing** — what stays local vs shared across sites; who owns the single procurement model and vendor master.

Each dimension is captured as `{current_state, future_state_questions}` — the future state is framed as **questions to resolve in a workshop**, not assumed answers.

## Lifecycle + value-stream mapping
Walk each stakeholder's **existing lifecycle** and identify where the new system changes inputs, outputs, participants, timing, and decisions at each stage. Complement the per-function lifecycle view with **cross-functional value streams** (e.g. concept-to-shelf, demand-to-fulfil, procure-to-pay) so the TOM doesn't re-encode the silos the single system is meant to remove.

## Prioritization
Rank functional areas by **people-impact density** (impact count per area from the CIA) and **severity**. Lead with the biggest people-change first. Deliberately drop or fold areas that are mostly technical/migration/data-handling with low people-change content — the TOM is about how *people* work.

## VRO — Value Realization Office (4 tiers)
A governance function that tracks whether the investment delivers its funded outcomes. Four tiers:

| Tier | Focus | Typical owner | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategic** | IRR / ROI against the business case | EPMO / Finance | Quarterly |
| **Operational** | Process-level KPIs (forecast accuracy, turns, landed cost) | Business Process Owners | Monthly |
| **Adoption** | Usage, training completion, workaround rate, confidence | Change Team | Bi-weekly during rollout |
| **Behavioral** | Are teams working differently? New meetings held, old workarounds retired | Change + Business Leads | Monthly |

Adoption leading indicators sit **alongside** the lagging financial benefits, as early signals a benefit line is on/off track. Design the VRO to live in business-as-usual from day one (capability transfer to an internal owner), not as an end-of-engagement hand-off.

## Roles / seats, value-at-risk, and AI
- **New roles & seats** — name the roles the model implies that don't exist today (e.g. shared-data steward, item-hierarchy chair, planning roles) and who should own them.
- **Value-at-risk** — each open/assumed fit-gap is a benefit that may not materialize; carry these as **leading risk indicators** alongside the benefits register.
- **AI role-shift** — where the new system's AI handles routine triage, roles shift from *producing* analysis to *reviewing and approving* system recommendations; treat that as explicit skills, governance (guardrails/override path), and benefit content — not a footnote.

## Standards anchoring
This method aligns to established practice: benefits realization (PMI Benefits Realization Management; APMG Managing Benefits) and operating-model design (the Operating Model Canvas; the POLISM dimensions). Cite lightly where it raises credibility with a sophisticated EPMO.

## Hard rules
- **Every claim traces to the CIA / transcript.** No fabricated counts, metrics, or dollar figures — a blank is correct.
- Impact counts come straight from the CIA's functional-area column; benefit targets stay blank until baselined (each KPI names a **baseline source + owner**).
- American English. Match the client's brand from `project.json`.
